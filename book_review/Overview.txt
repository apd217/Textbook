The headline findings
The book is good — genuinely. Paired-t, Power, Control, Multiple Comparisons, and Bootstrapping are better than commercial competitors. The problems cluster into four groups:

Book–lab mismatches (fix these first — they cost students points). The labs teach lmer(); your only mixed chapter teaches glmmTMB and skips the Week-10 "paired t ≡ lmer" demo entirely. The Week-11 lab runs the pooled t-test with formula syntax; the book leads Welch-only with vector syntax. R_Basics never teaches reading a CSV, factors, or table() — the Week 3 lab's whole agenda. The correlation chapter refuses the Cohen's-r benchmarks your centrally-written exams test.
Real bugs. A var(Z) that should be var(A) in echoed code; the chi-square chapter reports 124.14 three times when the math says 127.14; the income figure computes the mean from bin upper bounds while its caption claims midpoints; the one-sample chapter's apa() output is commented out but the next sentence refers to it; and the friendship-study chapters can't decide whether the DV is closeness points or a count of friends.
The old/new chapter divide. Your recently rewritten chapters (Paired-t, Power, the control trilogy, interactions) are the template. The slide-derived ones (One-Sample t, Covariance/Correlation, Chi-Square) have stub headings, unanswered lecture prompts ("Why is it the same number?" — never answered on the page), and hard-coded statistics. Chi-Square still says hypothesis testing is "coming later in the semester" — nine chapters after you taught it.
Missing connective tissue. Eight chapters lack "The Short Story"; only two chapters have self-check questions (Ch14's are perfect — I recommend propagating that exact pattern); zero figures have alt text; HTML toc-depth: 1 hides all section navigation on the web version; and the preface asks readers to report errors but gives no channel.
Ordering: two swaps recommended — Hierarchical before Control (fixes a broken "previous chapter" reference and puts the advanced-labeled chapter last), and Mixed Regression before the interaction chapters (Week 10 precedes Week 11). Details and a cheaper alternative are in the overview.

Also flagged: the preface promises "ANOVA gets a brief appearance" — with Advanced_Contrasts_ANOVA commented out, that appearance never happens anywhere. Item CH17-U01 is the cheap fix.

One suggestion beyond the checklist: item G04 (self-checks everywhere) is the highest-leverage new content for your audience, but the questions deserve your voice — consider having the implementing model draft candidates for you to punch up rather than approve blind.
